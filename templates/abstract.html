Project Abstract

Neurodegenerative diseases such as Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS), Parkinson's Disease (PD), and Huntington's Disease (HD) significantly impair motor function, making early detection crucial for timely intervention and improved patient outcomes.

In this study, we propose an optimized Stacking Ensemble machine learning model aimed at accurately classifying neurodegenerative diseases based on raw gait time-series data. Our research utilizes the PhysioNet Gait in Neurodegenerative Disease Database, comprising 15,160 individual strides from 64 unique patients.

Unlike previous approaches that suffer from data leakage by evaluating at the stride level, our methodology aggregates data to the patient level, calculating statistical metrics like Mean, Standard Deviation, and Coefficient of Variation (CV). We introduce novel engineered features, including a "Severity-Speed Ratio," to distinguish between clinically similar diseases.

Our Stacking Ensemble, combining Random Forest, XGBoost, and Gradient Boosting with balanced class weights, demonstrated a substantial accuracy increase. Validated using rigorous 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation, the model achieved a fair accuracy of 81.15%, successfully surpassing the baseline OPTIC paper's accuracy of 78.98% while eliminating class bias.

This study underscores the importance of patient-level aggregation and feature engineering, positioning our model as a robust, unbiased predictive tool for clinical gait analysis and early neurological screening.

Key Contributions:

  • Eliminated Data Leakage: Patient-level aggregation instead of stride-level evaluation
  • Novel Feature Engineering: Severity-Speed Ratio and asymmetry metrics
  • Balanced Predictions: Equal attention to all disease classes
  • Superior Performance: 81.15% accuracy vs 78.98% baseline
  • Clinical Applicability: Web-based deployment for real-world use
